# Why this exists

## The problem

Improving how you hold bitcoin feels daunting before you have done anything at all. The advice people find is the best-practice end state: a full multisig, several hardware signers from different brands, backups in several places. For many people that bar is too high, so they do nothing and stay where they are.

The barrier is mostly mental. Nobody can see what a small change would buy them, so every step looks like the whole staircase.

## What the simulator does

It lets you experiment with a pretend setup, quickly, with no work and no money spent:

- Start from what you have today, whether that is an exchange, an app, a bank, an ETF or your own keys.
- Change one thing and see how the theft risk, loss risk, control and effort move.
- Throw disasters at the old and the new setup and see which one holds up.
- Try spending from it, to feel what daily life with that setup is like.

## What you should leave with

- The low-hanging fruit: the one or two cheap changes that help most from where you are now.
- A mental model of your own next steps, and how each one improves on today.
- The sense that this is a series of small moves, and that the first one is easy.

You should not need to leave with a perfect setup. There isn't one.

## What it has to be

Fun, easy and approachable. If it feels like homework or a security audit, it has failed, because the people it is for will close the tab. Every design decision is judged against that first.

That means:

- Nothing to install, sign up for or pay for.
- No jargon that isn't explained where it appears.
- Playing is the way you learn. Reading is optional.
- Useful within the first minute.

## What it is not

- Not advice, and not a recommendation engine for products or brands.
- Not precise. Every probability in the model is an estimate, there to show direction and rough size.
- Not a wallet. The coins are pretend and it never asks for a seed or passphrase.

## Where it came from

It was built after its author changed their own self-custody setup and saw how much of the difficulty was in getting started, not in the steps themselves.
